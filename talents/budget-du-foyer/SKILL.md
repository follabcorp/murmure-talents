# Plan a household budget

Help the person turn their income and spending into a realistic monthly budget that covers essentials, savings and debt repayment. Use plain language and show the arithmetic. This is budgeting education, not licensed financial, tax or investment advice.

## Gather first

Ask only for what is missing:

1. Net (take-home) monthly income, and whether it varies from month to month.
2. Fixed expenses: rent or mortgage, utilities, insurance, subscriptions, loan minimums.
3. Variable expenses: groceries, transport, eating out, leisure.
4. Debts: balance, interest rate and minimum payment for each.
5. Goals: an emergency fund, a purchase, a debt-free date.
6. Currency, region and household size.

## Process

1. Total the income using the net amount. If income varies, budget on a cautious average or a low month.
2. List the expenses and sort them into Needs, Wants and Savings/Debt. Estimate honestly when statements are not available, and point out subscriptions and small leaks.
3. Suggest a method that fits the person: 50/30/20 for simplicity, zero-based (every unit of income gets a job) for control, or envelopes (a fixed amount per category) for someone who overspends.
4. Compare current spending with the chosen method and show the gaps ("Needs are 68% of income against a 50% target") so the pressure points are visible.
5. Build a small emergency buffer first, then work toward three to six months of essential expenses in an easily accessible account.
6. If there are debts, show two strategies and let the person choose: highest interest first (cheapest overall) or smallest balance first (most motivating).
7. Give every category a number so that income minus expenses, savings and debt payments equals zero (or respects the method's ratios). Cut Wants before Needs.
8. Make it sustainable: set savings aside on payday, suggest a short weekly money check-in and a monthly review, and keep a simple tracking table.

## Guardrails

- Include irregular costs (annual insurance, gifts, repairs) as small monthly set-asides.
- Avoid cuts that will collapse within a week; a budget the person can keep beats a perfect one.
- Never recommend specific investments, promise returns or give tax or legal advice. For investing, taxes, over-indebtedness or complex debt, suggest a qualified professional such as an accountant or a non-profit debt-advice service.
- Work only from the figures the person gives; do not open accounts, move money or contact anyone.

Reply in the language the person uses.
