# Outline a slideshow

Turn a topic, notes or a document into a clear slide-by-slide outline for a talk, a school or club presentation, a family event or a pitch. The result is an outline with speaker notes, not a finished visual deck: the person builds the slides in the tool they prefer.

## Three rules

1. One core message: the whole presentation exists to make one point land.
2. A story, not a list: tension and resolution, with a beginning, middle and end.
3. One idea per slide, with minimal text on the slide and the detail in the speaker notes.

## Process

1. Establish four anchors, asking at most three or four short questions: the audience and what they already know, what you want them to do or decide afterward, the one sentence they should remember tomorrow, and the constraints (time, number of slides, live or read alone, tone). If the person gives a document, propose answers and confirm the core message.
2. Write the core message as one clear sentence. Every slide must support it; cut the others.
3. Choose a simple arc: to persuade, alternate the problem and the better future and end with a request; to inform, give the answer first and then the supporting groups; to report, go from situation to problem to solution to next steps; to move people, go from the starting point to a turning point to the change.
4. Draft the spine: a hook (a question, a surprising figure or a scene), why it matters to this audience, the core message stated early, three to seven body slides, the proof (an example, a story, evidence), the request, and a memorable close. Allow about one to two minutes per content slide.
5. For each slide give a title written as a full statement ("The summer fair lost money because setup started too late", not "Finances"), the one idea, the little content to show (a phrase, a chart or an image idea), and a few sentences of speaker notes in spoken language.
6. Review: could someone read only the titles and follow the story? Remove any slide that does not move it forward. Suggest where an image or a chart would help.

## Guardrails

Do not invent statistics or sources; mark anything to verify. Do not take actions in any slide software or share files.

Reply in the language the person uses.
