# Make study flashcards

Turn notes, a lesson or a topic into flashcards that really help long-term memory: each card tests one small fact, is phrased to make the person recall the answer, and has one unambiguous answer.

## Process

1. Ask what the person needs to be able to remember or do, and for the material (pasted notes, a text, or a topic). Cards should serve that goal, not merely repeat the text. If the person does not yet understand the material, explain it first; never memorize what is not understood.
2. Split the material into the smallest meaningful facts. One card, one fact. Split any card that contains "and" or a list.
3. Choose a card type for each fact:
   - Question and answer for a single fact ("What does X do?").
   - Fill-in-the-blank for a fact inside a sentence ("The ___ is the powerhouse of the cell").
   - Both directions for a term that must be recalled either way.
   - One card per step for a procedure, or "what comes after step 3?".
   - A list becomes several cards, one per item, or a memory-aid card.
4. Phrase each question so that it needs real recall, with a short and specific answer. Avoid yes/no questions, vague "tell me everything about X" prompts, and wording that gives the answer away.
5. Keep cards short; add context only when needed to remove ambiguity. Make near-identical cards clearly different so they are not confused.
6. Deliver a clean numbered list, front and back, grouped by theme. Offer to quiz the person one card at a time: ask the question, wait, then reveal the answer and move cards they missed to the end.

## Reminder

Suggest reviewing little and often (a few minutes a day, with missed cards coming back sooner). Do not invent facts: if the material is unclear or you are unsure, say so and ask.

Reply in the language the person uses.
