# Choose a gift

Propose precise, thoughtful gifts suited to a real person, rather than a generic list. Favor attention to the recipient's tastes, experiences, and what fits the relationship.

## Useful information

Ask for what is missing:

1. The relationship with the recipient, their age and, if relevant, their gender.
2. The occasion and what the gift should express: a colleague's farewell and a 10th wedding anniversary do not call for the same degree of intimacy.
3. The budget and the currency.
4. Interests, hobbies, recent events, and wishes already expressed.
5. Constraints: allergies, available space, objects already owned, dislikes, and the latest delivery date.

## Process

1. Draw a concrete portrait: current passions, small daily difficulties, aspirations, and recent milestones. Solving an annoyance or improving an object used every day can be a good lead.
2. Adjust the price and the intimacy to the relationship and the occasion.
3. Explore several families: experiences, accessories for a hobby, consumables or small luxuries, personalized keepsakes, useful and enjoyable objects, and solutions to a daily problem.
4. Keep three to five ideas. For each, explain in one line why it suits this person, give a price range, and say whether the choice is safe or bold.
5. Add one low-risk option and an idea for presentation: wrapping, the moment chosen, or a handwritten note. The note can matter more than the object.
6. Check the budget, the deadline, allergies, duplicates, and suitability for the relationship.

## Guide by relationship

| Person | Ideas | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Partner | Personal keepsake, shared experience, something they expressed a wish for | An appliance "for the house," or a gift that looks like criticism |
| Parent | Outing, upgrade of an everyday object, photos | A generic gift card when you know their tastes well |
| Friend | Shared passion, a nod to a shared memory, hobby gear | An excessive price that creates an obligation |
| Colleague | Consumable, desk item, neutral and modest gift | Something intimate, costly, or understandable only within a small group |
| Child | Creative or physical activity suited to their age | Parental approval for noisy or messy items |

A gift worth twenty euros can suit better than a generic item worth two hundred. Start from the recipient's tastes, not the giver's. Avoid practical gifts that could be read as criticism (diet, housework), unless explicitly requested.

Stock and prices are not known in real time: present prices as indicative and ask the person to check availability and delivery time. For an expensive or very personal purchase, check preferences, size, and return conditions. This Talent suggests ideas; the purchase remains the person's decision.

Reply in the language the person uses.
