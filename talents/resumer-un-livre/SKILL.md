# Summarize a book

Give a faithful, well-organized summary of a book at the depth the person wants, so they can decide whether to read it, remember it, apply it, or prepare a discussion.

## Accuracy first

Only summarize a book you actually know. If you are unsure about the title, the edition or the content, say so and ask the person to paste text or confirm details. Never invent quotes, figures, characters or plot points. A summary does not replace reading when nuance matters or when the person must cite the book.

## Gather first

1. The book: title and author, confirmed.
2. Depth: one sentence, about a page, key takeaways, or chapter by chapter.
3. Purpose: decide whether to read it, apply it, recall it, study it, or run a book club.
4. For fiction, whether spoilers are fine.

## Process

1. Find the spine. For non-fiction: the central thesis and the three to seven big ideas that support it. For fiction: premise, central conflict, themes and arc, respecting the spoiler preference.
2. Write at the requested depth: a one-line message; a page with the thesis, key ideas and why it matters; a ranked list of takeaways; or two to four lines per chapter that state each chapter's point rather than just listing it.
3. Keep the one or two most memorable examples or scenes behind each idea: concrete beats abstract.
4. For non-fiction, turn the ideas into concrete things the person could try.
5. Stay faithful and neutral. Present the author's argument accurately first; keep "what the book says" separate from "what I think", and mention where the book is contested.
6. On request, add a few discussion questions or key terms.

## Match the output to the goal

Deciding whether to read: one sentence, three takeaways, and who it suits. Applying it: takeaways as actions. Recalling: a short summary with memorable examples. Studying: chapter notes with a reminder to check the book itself. Book club: themes and five to eight open questions.

Reply in the language the person uses.
