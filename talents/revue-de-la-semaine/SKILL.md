# Run a weekly review

Guide a short weekly reset: look back at the week, see what is pending or slipping, and choose a few realistic outcomes for the week ahead. Work only with what the person tells you or pastes in (calendar entries, to-do lists, notes, reminders). You cannot see their calendar or accounts, and you change nothing in them.

## Process

1. Set the scope. Ask for the time zone, the week to review, and what they have to hand. Offer a simple way to paste it: a list of last week's events and next week's, current to-dos, loose notes. Say you will propose, not change, anything.
2. Review the past week: what got done, meetings and promises made, and follow-ups they imply. Note the wins first.
3. Look at the next one or two weeks: fixed commitments, deadlines, trips and preparations. Spot clashes and overload.
4. Clear the loose ends. For each item in notes or lists, suggest one of: a next action, part of a project, waiting on someone, scheduled for a date, someday, or drop. State how many items remain unsorted.
5. For each ongoing project (a trip, a move, a school application, a renovation), name the desired result, the next concrete action and the date, any blocker, and flag projects with no next action or with contradictory status.
6. List promises the person made and things others owe them. Suggest follow-ups with a date and a channel. Do not assume that silence means done.
7. Build a plan that fits the real time available: after the fixed commitments, choose a small number of outcomes for the week and the next actions, ranked by consequence, deadline and effort. Do not fill every free hour, and say what is deliberately postponed.

## Output

1. Wins and what was completed. 2. Overdue or at risk. 3. Waiting and follow-ups. 4. Stalled or unclear projects. 5. Next week's outcomes and constraints. 6. Questions or gaps in what you were given.

## Guardrails

Do not delete, reschedule or send anything on the person's behalf; drafts of messages are suggestions only. Do not carry every unfinished item forward as top priority. Be kind about what slipped.

Reply in the language the person uses.
