# Build a habit

Turn a vague intention into a concrete behavior: a trigger, a routine, a reward, an environment, and tracking able to withstand a lapse. Make wanted habits visible, easy, attractive, and satisfying; do the reverse for those the person wants to give up.

This Talent supports everyday changes: reading, activity, sleep, concentration, or screen time. For an addiction, self-harm, an eating disorder, or compulsions causing real harm, recommend a qualified professional; a habit chart is not treatment.

## Useful information

Gather the precise, observable behavior; what to build or give up, and why; the moment and place where it could happen; the obstacles; past attempts; existing routines; and the real capacity in time and energy.

## Process

1. Shrink the behavior to a very small action: one page rather than a book, one sentence rather than a chapter. Aim first for regularity, then increase. Link the action to a chosen identity: "I am a person who reads," rather than a single number.
2. Find a reliable trigger: a time, a place, or a preceding action. Attach the action to an established routine: "after I make my coffee, I write one sentence."
3. Adjust the environment: a book on the pillow, workout clothes laid out, the phone in another room. Make the desired action visible and easy, and the unwanted action less visible and harder.
4. Plan an immediate satisfaction: a checkmark, a small celebration, or an association with a pleasant activity.
5. To give up a habit, make its trigger less visible, complicate the routine, and remove the reward. Choose a replacement that meets the same need.
6. Propose a simple visual tracker kept by the person. Plan a recovery rule: one missed day does not cancel the effort; try not to miss two opportunities in a row. Prepare a plan: "if this obstacle arises, then I will do this minimal version."
7. Schedule a weekly review: did the action happen, and what got in the way? Change the trigger, the size, or the environment, without judging the person's worth. Increase only when the action has become regular and easy.

## Adjusting the plan

| Difficulty | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Repeated forgetting | Rely on a very reliable existing routine |
| Lack of time | Reduce to a two-minute version |
| Fluctuating motivation | Ease the environment and make the reward immediate |
| Enthusiastic start, then giving up | Plan the recovery and a conditional plan |
| Repeated action without an anchor | Link it to the person they want to become |

Start only one new habit at a time. Avoid over-ambition in the first week, relying on willpower alone, vague triggers, all-or-nothing thinking, and the absence of reward. Tracking is an aid, not proof of personal failure. The review is an appointment proposed within the plan; this Talent creates neither reminder nor automatic monitoring.
