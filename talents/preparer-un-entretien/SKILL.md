# Prepare for an interview

Prepare the likely questions, the stories drawn from lived experiences, the difficult answers, and the questions to ask. Offer a practice interview with feedback if the person wishes. No experience is invented.

## Useful information

Gather the role, the company, and the offer; the stage and format of the interview; the main projects and achievements; the concerns (career break, career change, limited experience, stage fright); and whether the person wants a preparation plan or an immediate practice session.

## Process

1. From the offer and the stage, suggest about five questions on behavior and experience, three specific to the role, and the classics: self-introduction, motivation, and weak points.
2. Prepare four to six lived stories using **STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result**. For each, note the skills shown: responsibility, conflict, failure, ambiguity, and the effect produced. One story can answer several questions with a different emphasis.
3. Build "Tell me about yourself" in **60 to 90 seconds**: present, relevant past, then the future sought. Avoid a full autobiography.
4. Prepare the difficult questions: a real weakness and the measure taken, the reason for leaving, a career break, a specific and genuine motivation. For salary, first prepare a request for a framework and then, if appropriate, a range based on the information provided and dated.
5. Prepare four to six questions about the role, the team, the success criteria, and the challenges. Avoid questions whose answers are already in a basic presentation of the company.
6. For a practice session, ask **one question at a time**, wait for the answer, then give precise feedback on structure, examples, length, and filler words. Return to the answer if needed.
7. End with logistics: format, time and time zone, dress, people met, warm-up, and a plan for the thank-you note.

## Adapting the practice

| Interview | Priorities |
| --- | --- |
| First recruitment call | Self-introduction, motivation, salary, and logistics |
| Hiring manager | STAR stories linked to the team's real problems |
| Technical | Skills for the role, reasoning spoken aloud, examples of difficulties solved |
| Several interviewers | Collaboration, conflict, influence without authority |
| Final stage or leadership | Vision, motivation, contribution to the team, long-term goals |

This Talent works on structure, narrative, and how to answer. Specialized technical preparation may need additional resources; it does not install or invoke another Skill.

For an experience-based answer, aim for **90 seconds to two minutes**. Place the result at the beginning or the end, without burying it, with numbers only if they are real. Practice aloud and memorize the steps rather than the exact text. Avoid vague answers, digressions, false weaknesses, criticism of a former employer, and having no final questions.

Reply in the language the person uses.
