# Cover letter

Write a letter that complements the CV rather than repeating it. Answer three questions quickly: why this company, why this role, and why this person, with one or two concrete proofs.

It also suits explaining a career change, a career break, or an unusual fit. If little specific material is available, three precise paragraphs are better than a generic page. Use verifiable facts, without inventing enthusiasm or information about the company.

## Useful information

Gather the job offer and the company name; two or three relevant achievements, quantified if possible; a genuine reason for wanting this role; the points to explain (career change, break, move, qualification); and the expected register, formal or professional and warm.

## Process

1. Find in the information provided a true detail about the company that the person connects with: a product, a mission, a launch, or a problem addressed. If research would be needed, ask the person for the source. This detail serves as the hook, rather than a generic opening formula.
2. Identify the three main requirements of the offer and link them to the real achievements that answer them.
3. Write a two- or three-sentence opening: name the role and start with the hook or the strongest proof.
4. Write one or two short paragraphs, each with a statement of fit supported by an example and its concrete effect. Do not turn the CV's list of duties into prose.
5. If needed, explain the break, career change, or move in one confident sentence, looking toward what comes next.
6. Close in two sentences: interest in the role, availability, an offer to talk, and thanks.
7. Aim for about **250 to 350 words**, under one page. Remove any sentence that could appear unchanged in another application.
8. Adapt the layout to the medium: email body or attached letter. If the context calls for a German-speaking format, include sender and recipient details, date, and subject line as a formal letter requires.

## Choosing the emphasis

| Situation | Highlight |
| --- | --- |
| Direct fit with the role | The most relevant quantified achievement |
| Career change | Transferable skills and a deliberate, not random, change |
| Career break | One sentence looking forward, then the value brought |
| Ambitious role at a target company | Genuine motivation and proven ability to learn quickly |
| Referral | The name of the person who recommended the application, if use is authorized |

Favor proof over claims ("improved conversion by 40%," only if that figure is real). Show what the person brings, not only what they hope to receive. The tone should stay confident, warm, and concise. Address the letter to a named person when the name is available. Check spelling, the company name, and any traces of an earlier application. The letter remains a draft to review; no application is sent.

Reply in the language the person uses.
