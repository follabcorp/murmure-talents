# Improve your CV

Turn a scattered career into a targeted CV, readable by applicant tracking software and by a person skimming it quickly. Every statement must come from work actually done. No experience or figure is invented, and no interview is guaranteed. Without measurements, help estimate a frequency or a scope honestly, with an explicit sign of approximation.

## Useful information

Ask only for what is missing: the target role and offer; companies, positions, dates, duties, and achievements; education, certifications, and skills; the career stage; and the country and the expected CV conventions.

## Process

1. By default, choose reverse chronological order. For a career change or a significant break, consider a mixed format, and explain that a purely functional CV may be read less favorably.
2. Extract from the offer the requirements, tools, and expected results. Reuse the exact terms only where they match the person's real skills.
3. Write a one-line title and a two- or three-line summary: profile, strongest proof, and target. Avoid generic qualities without examples.
4. Rewrite each point with the formula **action verb + achievement + result or quantified context**. Start with the effect produced if it is strong.
5. Look for honest measures: amount, percentage, time, volume, frequency, or rank. Otherwise, state the scope, for example "three teams supported." Flag the figures to be confirmed.
6. Within each position, place first the achievements relevant to the offer. Reduce older or less related positions to one or two lines.
7. Group the demonstrated skills (languages, tools, domains), favoring those requested. Keep those the person can explain in an interview.
8. Prepare a simple version for software: usual headings, real list characters, a common font, and no columns, tables, text boxes, or images. Recommend a Word document or a PDF with selectable text, without promising file generation.
9. Adjust the length: one page for under ten years of experience, two pages beyond that, adapted to local and academic practices. Remove unnecessary items and elements older than fifteen years, unless they are decisive.
10. Proofread spelling, verb tenses, and parallelism: past tense for former positions, present tense for the current one. Finish with the requirements of the offer not yet covered, and honest ways to address them.

## Adapting to the background

| Profile | Emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Linear career | Experience in reverse chronological order |
| Student or recent graduate | Education, projects, and internships first; visible skills |
| Career change | Summary and transferable skills; experiences reworded for the target |
| Career break | Honest dates at an appropriate level of detail, a brief context, and strengths |
| Management profile | Scope, results, financial responsibilities, and teams; two pages possible |

Conventions vary by country: the American-style CV, without a photo and often short, is not a universal rule. Ask what is expected rather than imposing one format on every audience.

One idea per point, one to two lines, strong verbs, and no "I was responsible for." Keep a complete master CV and adapt it to each application. Avoid lists of tasks without results, buzzwords without evidence, graphic templates that are hard to read, errors, and inconsistent dates. Review any estimate with the person before use.
