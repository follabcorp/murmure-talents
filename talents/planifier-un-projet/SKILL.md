# Plan a personal project

Turn a goal such as moving house, organizing an event, renovating a room or preparing a trip into a clear plan: the steps, their order, rough durations, milestones and the main risks. The method is the same for any project.

## Gather first

Ask at most three to five sharp questions about what is missing:

1. What "done" looks like: the final result.
2. The deadline, if any, and fixed dates imposed by others (a booking, a delivery, a lease end).
3. Who can help and how much time per week the person has.
4. Budget and other constraints.

## Process

1. Break the goal into main blocks by result (for example "new flat found", "move booked", "utilities transferred"), then into small tasks. A task should start with a verb, have a clear "done" criterion and fit in a few hours to a couple of days; split anything bigger or anything too vague to estimate.
2. Mark milestones: moments that mark real progress, such as "keys collected" or "all invitations sent".
3. Find dependencies: what must be finished before another task can start, and what can run in parallel. Fixed external dates come first.
4. Estimate each task with a range (quickest, most likely, slowest), and keep effort (hours of work) separate from duration (calendar days, affected by waiting times and availability).
5. Build a timeline backward from the deadline if there is one. Identify the longest chain of dependent tasks: any delay there delays everything, so start it early.
6. Add a safety margin of roughly 15 to 30 percent to the whole project rather than padding every task.
7. List the three to five main risks (late delivery, bad weather, someone unavailable), how likely each is, and a back-up action.
8. Present the plan as a short table: task, who, estimate, depends on, target date. Offer to turn the next two weeks into a simple checklist and to re-plan when something changes.

## Guardrails

Use realistic, honest estimates, and say so when information is missing. For work that involves safety, building regulations, contracts or large sums, recommend a qualified professional. Do not book, buy or contact anyone.

Reply in the language the person uses.
