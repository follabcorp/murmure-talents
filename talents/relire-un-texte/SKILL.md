# Proofread a text

Correct and polish the person's text for spelling, grammar, punctuation, clarity and consistency, without changing their meaning, voice or intent. It suits emails, letters, essays, reports, CVs, posts and school or work documents.

## Cardinal rule

Preserve meaning. If a change could shift the meaning, emphasis, tone or the author's voice, offer it as a suggestion instead of applying it silently. If a sentence can be read two ways, do not guess: show both readings and ask.

## Process

1. Check the scope: who will read the text, how formal it is, and which regional spelling or convention to follow (for instance British or American English, or the person's variety of French). Keep the dominant style already present and mention any assumption.
2. First pass, correctness: spelling, typos, agreement, tense, pronouns, articles, prepositions, punctuation, apostrophes, hyphens. Fix these directly.
3. Second pass, clarity: ambiguous or unclear sentences, misplaced modifiers, unclear pronouns, overlong sentences. Use the active voice only when it keeps the emphasis.
4. Third pass, concision: remove filler and repetition, and replace wordy phrases with shorter ones, without losing nuance or politeness.
5. Fourth pass, consistency: one spelling variety, consistent capitalization, numbers, dates, quotation marks and terminology.
6. Return the corrected text first. Then give a short list of changes in two groups: fixes applied (objective) and suggestions the person should approve (judgement calls), quoting the original wording next to each suggestion.

## Fix or suggest

Fix: spelling, typos, agreement, clear punctuation errors, obvious filler. Suggest: word choices that affect tone, reordering that changes emphasis, passive-to-active changes that shift responsibility. Flag, never silently change: a possible factual error, a figure or date that contradicts another. Leave alone: stylistic choices with no clear rule, and deliberate effects such as a short fragment.

Do not over-edit: if a sentence is correct and clear, keep it. Keep the author's expressions and personality. Preserve formatting and line breaks.

Reply in the language the person uses.
