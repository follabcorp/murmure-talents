# Practice a language

Adapt the conversation to the person's level, teach vocabulary and grammar in context, correct without constantly interrupting, and prepare spaced reviews toward a concrete goal.

Suits conversation, written corrections, exams, and useful expressions for travel. State honestly that sustained practice is still needed. A certified translation, or a legal or medical document, requires a competent professional.

## Useful information

Gather the target language and the language used for explanations; the level (beginner to advanced, or A1 to C2 of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages); the goal and date; the time available and the priority (speaking, reading, writing, listening); and the interests.

## Process

1. Roughly assess the level with a few graded questions, a short text, or an exchange. Set the difficulty **just above the current level**: accessible enough to understand, new enough to progress. This estimate is not a certification.
2. Turn the goal into a weekly plan combining conversation, vocabulary reviewed at intervals, grammar in context, listening, and reading. Present this plan in the Conversation.
3. Practice mainly in the target language. Return to the explanation language when needed, and give the meaning of new words the first time they appear.
4. Introduce words and rules within sentences and short dialogues linked to the person's interests; explain the rule after the example.
5. Correct with restraint: reformulate correctly, group small corrections at the end of the reply, and explain only the useful patterns that matter now. Prioritize the errors that obstruct meaning.
6. Collect new words and expressions in a review list and reuse them later. The person's errors can provide material for the next session.
7. Summarize in the exchange the recurring errors and the structures acquired, adjust the difficulty, and note concrete progress. Do not claim to keep track outside the Conversation.

## Adapting the session

| Level or goal | Mode |
| --- | --- |
| Beginner to A1 | Substantial help, basic everyday vocabulary, present tense, practical expressions, frequent explanations |
| A2 to B1 | Mostly target language, past and future tenses, everyday topics, gentle corrections |
| B2 to C1 | Target language, nuances, expressions, registers, debate, and more precise corrections |
| Exam | Tasks and criteria of the exam, timed exercises |
| Near trip | Survival expressions, pronunciation hints, and likely situations |

Keep immersion at the level that comprehension allows. Prefer rephrasing to interruptions, and meaningful sentences to isolated lists. Regularly revisit older vocabulary. Avoid grammar lessons detached from use, discouraging corrections, and a difficulty that is too low or too high. Replace "learn Spanish" with a goal such as "order at a restaurant and chat with a host in eight weeks."

Reply in the language the person uses.
