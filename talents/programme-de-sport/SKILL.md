# Plan a gentle exercise routine

Design a simple, progressive exercise routine around the person's goal, current level, available time, equipment and limits. Favor regularity and enjoyment over performance. This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice.

## Safety first

- Before starting, say once and clearly that the person should check with a doctor if they have an injury, a heart or blood-pressure condition, are pregnant, take regular medication, or are returning after a long break. Refer rehabilitation questions to a physiotherapist or doctor.
- Never promise a health outcome or a weight result. Never suggest extreme diets or training through sharp pain: pain, dizziness or chest discomfort means stop and seek advice.

## Gather first

Ask only for what is missing:

1. One main goal (feel more energetic, build strength, improve stamina, move more daily, general health).
2. Starting level and recent activity.
3. Days per week and minutes per session that are realistic.
4. Equipment: none, a few dumbbells or bands, a gym, a bike, walking routes.
5. Limits: injuries, age, joint problems, recovery, and what the person enjoys or hates.

## Process

1. Restate the goal and set honest expectations: noticeable changes take weeks, not days.
2. Choose a structure that fits the time available. A beginner does well with two or three full-body sessions a week; add walking or easy cardio on other days.
3. Pick simple movements covering the body: a squat or sit-to-stand, a hinge such as a hip bridge, a push, a pull, a carry or plank, plus walking. Offer an easier and a harder variant of each, matched to the equipment.
4. Give sets, repetitions and rest in plain terms (for example "2 sets of 10, rest 1 minute"), starting deliberately light.
5. Define progression: add a few repetitions, then a little load or time, only when form stays good. Plan an easier week every four to six weeks.
6. Lay out the week with training days, rest days and a short warm-up (five minutes of easy movement) and cool-down.
7. Offer a simple log (date, exercises, how it felt) and a check-in after two to three weeks to adjust.

## Guardrails

Do not give calorie targets or diet plans; for nutrition questions suggest a registered dietitian or doctor. Do not diagnose. Do not book classes or purchase equipment.

Reply in the language the person uses.
