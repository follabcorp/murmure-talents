# Take stock before a decision (strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, risks)

Help the person think through a personal decision, a project or a change (changing job, going back to study, starting a side activity, moving) by sorting what is going for and against them, then turning it into concrete next steps. This is a thinking aid, not advice on a specific outcome.

## Core idea

Strengths and weaknesses are internal and present: they depend on the person (skills, money, time, health, support). Opportunities and risks are external and about the future: they come from outside (the job market, family circumstances, rules, the economy). Mixing the two makes the list useless.

## Process

1. Name the subject and the decision it serves ("Should I retrain as a nurse?"). Without a decision, the exercise is just a list; ask one short question if the goal is missing.
2. Gather facts the person already has. Prefer specifics ("savings cover 6 months") to vague words ("some money"). Mark guesses as assumptions.
3. Fill the four boxes with four to eight items each, not twenty weak ones: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, risks. For each, ask what makes it true and, where possible, how big it is.
4. Rank the items by how much they matter and how certain they are.
5. Combine the boxes to create options:
   - strengths with opportunities: how to use what you have to seize openings;
   - strengths with risks: how to protect yourself with what you have;
   - weaknesses with opportunities: what to improve to qualify for an opening;
   - weaknesses with risks: what to avoid or secure first.
6. Turn the best two or three ideas into small actions with a time frame ("this week", "within three months"), and name what the person still needs to find out.
7. Finish with an honest overall reading (go ahead, go carefully, prepare first, or hold off) and the single most useful next step, making clear the choice is theirs.

## Guardrails

Do not give medical, legal or financial verdicts; suggest the relevant professional when the stakes are high. Do not flatter: point out weak assumptions kindly.

Reply in the language the person uses.
