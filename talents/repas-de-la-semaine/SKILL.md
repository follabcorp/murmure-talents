# Plan the week's meals

Build a realistic weekly menu with a consolidated shopping list and an optional batch-cooking plan, based on the household's tastes, time and budget. This is meal planning, not nutritional therapy.

## Gather first

Ask two to four focused questions, then proceed with sensible defaults instead of stalling:

1. Who eats, and how many portions per meal (adults, children).
2. Diet, allergies and dislikes. Treat allergies as hard constraints: never substitute an allergen, and mention cross-contamination risks.
3. Weekly budget and currency.
4. Time available on weeknights, kitchen equipment, cooking confidence, and whether a weekend prep session is welcome.
5. Which meals to plan (breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks) and for how many days.

## Process

1. Pick a few anchor ingredients for the week (two or three proteins or protein-rich staples such as eggs and legumes, two or three starches, a rotating set of vegetables) so one shopping trip covers everything and little goes to waste.
2. Reduce decisions: one or two repeating breakfasts, two or three lunches (often dinner leftovers), four or five distinct dinners. Keep one flexible slot for eating out or using up leftovers.
3. Draw a day-by-meal table. Keep weeknight dinners quick and put longer recipes on days with more time.
4. Make leftovers intentional: cook a double batch of dinner to become the next day's lunch.
5. If the person wants a batch-cooking session, group the tasks (one tray of roasted vegetables, one pot of grains, one sauce), order them so the oven and hobs are never idle, and say what goes in the fridge or freezer and for how long.
6. Write the shopping list: add up quantities across meals, round to buyable units ("3 onions", not "half an onion six times"), group by store section, and list pantry staples separately so they are not bought twice.
7. Deliver in this order: assumptions, the weekly table, the prep plan, the shopping list by section, then swap ideas and storage tips.

## Guardrails

Do not calculate calorie or macro targets and do not prescribe a diet. For a medical condition (diabetes, kidney disease, an eating disorder, pregnancy-related needs) suggest a doctor or a registered dietitian. Prefer seasonal and frozen produce when cost matters. Do not place orders or open any account.

Reply in the language the person uses.
