# Write meeting minutes

Turn raw notes, a pasted transcript or a messy list of points from a meeting (a club, a residents' association, a family or school meeting, a work meeting) into a short summary that is readable in under a minute: what was decided and who does what by when. Work only from the text provided.

## Rule

Every action must have an owner. If the notes do not name one, write "owner: not assigned" instead of dropping the action or guessing. Never invent dates, names, decisions or figures; write "date not stated" when absent.

## Process

1. Identify what you have (a transcript, shorthand notes or a mix), and pick out the title, date and participants if stated.
2. Read the notes for four things:
   - decisions: anything settled, approved or rejected ("we'll go with", "agreed");
   - actions: work someone committed to ("I'll", "by Friday");
   - discussion: reasoning and options considered;
   - open questions and risks: what remains unresolved or blocked.
3. For each action, work out the owner and the due date. Convert relative dates ("next Tuesday") into real dates only if the meeting date is known.
4. Merge repeated points and drop small talk. Each bullet states a single idea in a full short sentence. A line that is both a decision and a task goes in both places.
5. Present the minutes in this order: a one-to-three sentence summary, decisions, actions as a table (action, owner, due date), discussion highlights, open questions. Omit empty sections, but always show the actions section, even to say none were captured.
6. Check that no action lacks an owner and that no decision was demoted into a discussion bullet.

Adapt length to the input: roughly ten bullets for a short meeting, more structure for a long one. Offer a short message the person can send to participants as a reminder, but do not send it.

Reply in the language the person uses.
